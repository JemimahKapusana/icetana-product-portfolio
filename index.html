Idea & Opportunity

Exploring the mining industry as a potential new customer market for icetana AI's existing video analytics technology.

IN THE MEETING

LEADERSHIP What opportunities should we explore for future growth?

MY IDEA I think the mining industry is worth investigating. Here's why.

“

THE THINKING BEHIND MY SUGGESTION

Three reasons I'd explore mining.

01

A major market in our backyard

Western Australia has a large mining industry. Mining companies invest heavily in operations, equipment and technology. That makes it an interesting market to research, even if it doesn't guarantee demand for our software.

WA Government industry data ↗
02

A possible fit for video AI

Mining companies operate large, often remote sites where safety, security and monitoring matter. icetana already uses video AI to identify unusual activity. I'd investigate which mining problems that expertise could help solve.

icetana AI features ↗
03

Build on what already exists

Instead of assuming we need a brand-new product, I'd investigate whether parts of icetana's existing technology could be adapted to mining. That might reduce time and cost, but we'd need engineering to confirm what's realistic.

A possibility to test, not a promise.
✦

My first move wouldn't be to build anything. It would be to find out which mining problems are worth solving, who might pay to solve them and whether icetana has a genuine advantage.

LEAD 01NOT PURSUED

My first idea:
vehicle collision monitoring.

I started with a serious safety issue: interactions between heavy mining equipment and smaller vehicles.

WorkSafe WA warned that mines were reporting collisions, rollovers and uncontrolled equipment movements too often. In March 2026, it also reported recent light-vehicle rollovers that had caused a fatality and serious injuries.

WHY I PARKED THIS IDEA

The safety problem is real. The competition is already sophisticated.

Hexagon launched its next-generation Vehicle Intervention System in September 2026, including intervention capabilities for certain hazardous situations. Caterpillar also offers mining collision-awareness and proximity systems. A standalone camera alert would need a very compelling advantage to compete.

My decision: Don't recommend this as our first direction without a clearer unmet need and strong safety evidence.

THE OPERATIONAL PROBLEM

A small object can cause a big shutdown.

01

The problem

Foreign objects can travel with ore and reach crushers or other processing equipment. If they're not caught early, they can cause damage, disruption and costly downtime. Continuous manual visual checks can also be difficult and expose workers to risk.

02

The idea

Use cameras and computer vision to recognise possible foreign objects as ore moves along a conveyor. Alert operators early enough to investigate and respond.

03

Why it matters

Fewer unplanned interruptions, less equipment damage and safer responses could create meaningful value. These are potential benefits to test, not results from my own model.

IS BHP THE ONLY ONE?

I looked beyond BHP.

These cases show industrial interest in camera-based conveyor monitoring. They do not prove a new icetana product would win customers.

SEFOREIGN-OBJECT DETECTION

Boliden Sweden

The problem: Metal parts could enter the ore stream and damage equipment. An earlier laser-camera solution triggered false alarms when dust-suppression water hit the ore.

The response: Boliden worked with Norrin on AI vision that identifies foreign objects and can stop the belt. Norrin reports that related software was also deployed at other Boliden sites.

Read Norrin's Boliden case study ↗
RUFOREIGN-OBJECT DETECTION

Norilsk Nickel Russia

The problem: Unwanted objects in the ore stream can threaten processing equipment.

The response: In October 2026, Norilsk Nickel and Reksoft reported deploying AI video analytics that detects foreign objects on conveyors and supports an operational response.

Read Reksoft's October 2026 announcement ↗
AURELATED CONVEYOR VISION

Anglo American Queensland

The problem: At Dawson Mine, clips joining the 27 km conveyor could fail, leading to belt damage and repairs that took days.

The response: A camera-and-AI approach let technicians review the clips remotely in about five minutes.

Different problem: this detects failing belt clips, not foreign objects. It still shows another practical use of conveyor vision.

Read the 2023 Dawson Mine story ↗

THE PART THAT NEEDS HONESTY

The problem exists.
But is there room for us?

Real examples are encouraging. They are not the same thing as evidence that another mining operator would buy a new solution from icetana.

THE COMPETITION

We wouldn't be first.

Mines already use metal detectors, magnets and other protective systems. Australian supplier Scantech also offers SIZESCAN FOD, which uses cameras and machine learning to detect foreign objects in conveyed material.

My conclusion: “Camera-only” isn't enough of a competitive advantage.

THE CUSTOMER HYPOTHESIS

Who might need this?

I'd investigate mid-sized mining and processing operations that:

  • Experience recurring conveyor-related interruptions.
  • Already have suitable cameras in place.
  • Don't have an internal computer vision team.
  • Would prefer a supported solution over building one from scratch.

These are potential customer characteristics to test, not confirmed buyer needs.

?

THE QUESTION I'D TAKE INTO THE NEXT MEETING

Can icetana offer something customers can't already get easily?

That might mean faster installation, lower total cost, easier integration, more reliable alerts or a better operator experience. But each of those advantages needs evidence.

MY RECOMMENDATION · AT THIS STAGE

Investigate further.
Don't commit to building yet.

I'd recommend direct interviews with mining operators and processing teams, a deeper review of competing products, and a small technical feasibility study only if it helps answer a useful question.

01Speak to real customers

How often does the problem happen? What do current systems miss?

02Test the commercial gap

What would make switching or buying worthwhile?

03Check technical fit

Could icetana deliver this reliably and cost-effectively?

Where the real research ends

That is a learning assumption, not a market finding or real icetana decision.
